Jason P. Londo

Principal Investigator · Cornell University

I joined the Horticulture Section faculty in 2022 as an associate professor of fruit crop physiology and climate adaptation. Previously, I was a Research Geneticist with the USDA-ARS Grape Genetics Unit, located at Cornell AgriTech in Geneva, New York.

My extension program goals are to provide research driven-tools and information to the New York fruit crop industry focusing on adapting crops to climate-change-induced environmental stress. I work collaboratively with other faculty members, Cornell Cooperative Extension, and stakeholders to identify climate induced disorders, develop mitigation methods, and improve the resilience and sustainability of fruit crop production through applied research.

I manage the Cornell grapevine cold hardiness monitoring program and associated website. My current research is focusing on understanding aspects of winter hardiness and spring deacclimation in apples and grapes to identify the rootstocks and scion combinations that provide the greatest resistance to early winter warming conditions.

Brandon G. Roy, PhD

Postdoctoral Researcher · Cornell University

Brandon completed his B.S. in Biochemistry and Molecular Biology at Lebanon Valley College in Annville, Pennsylvania. He first came to Geneva, NY as a Cornell AgriTech Summer Scholar in 2019, and joined the lab of Dr. Marc Fuchs within the Plant Pathology and Plant-Microbe Biology Section. Brandon has developed a passion for molecular benchwork and computational biology to understand host and pathogen interactions through RNA-Seq, genomics, and proteomics. He joined the Londo Lab in May 2025 as a post-doc where he is investigating the molecular components of grapevine climate adaptation.

Kenneth Buck

PhD Candidate · Cornell University

Kenneth is a PhD candidate finishing his thesis on rootstock × scion effects on apple bud cold hardiness. His work integrates electrolyte leakage assays, multispectral aerial drone imaging, and RNAseq across multiple growing seasons in New York apple orchards, asking how rootstock genotype shapes scion bud hardiness and whether those effects are stable from season to season.

Jennifer Russo

PhD Student · Cornell University

Jennifer is an extension-focused PhD student investigating vineyard floor management, cover crops, and microclimate effects in New York vineyards. Her work bridges applied management practices with the physiological responses they drive — connecting cover-crop and floor-management decisions to grapevine cold hardiness, frost risk, and fruit ripening outcomes.

Sarah Overbeck

PhD Student · Cornell University

Sarah is a PhD student investigating the genetics of apple shoot and root architecture. Using a columnar × weeping progeny population grown at Cornell AgriTech, she is testing whether the genetic signals that shape above-ground tree architecture are recapitulated in root system architecture — combining branch- and leaf-angle phenotyping with root anatomy and molecular markers for the Co (columnar) and We (weeping) loci.

Davis Upchurch

PhD Student · Cornell University

Davis is a PhD student studying viral impacts on apple growth and production. His work centers on Apple stem grooving virus (ASGV), using in situ hybridization to localize virus in apple tissues, QTL mapping to identify rootstock resistance loci, and comparative physiology of virus-clean versus virus-infected trees.
